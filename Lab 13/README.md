# The Architecture of Dimensionality: Hedonic Pricing & the FWL Theorem

## Objective
Construct and validate a multivariate hedonic pricing model on 2026 California real estate data to empirically demonstrate the Frisch-Waugh-Lovell (FWL) Theorem, proving that algorithmic partialling-out replicates exact OLS coefficients and corrects for omitted variable bias in property valuation.

---

## Data
- **Source:** Zillow synthetic dataset (2026 California residential real estate)
- **Key Variables:** `Sale_Price`, `Property_Age`, `Distance_to_Tech_Hub`
- **Stack:** Python 3.10+, `pandas`, `statsmodels.formula.api`, `matplotlib`

---

## Methodology

- **Baseline OLS (Bivariate):** Regressed `Sale_Price` on `Property_Age` alone to establish a naive pricing benchmark, deliberately omitting confounding spatial covariates.
- **Multivariate OLS (Full Model):** Re-specified the model to include `Distance_to_Tech_Hub`, isolating each regressor's independent marginal contribution to sale price under *ceteris paribus* conditions.
- **Manual FWL Residual Extraction:** Partialled out `Distance_to_Tech_Hub` by regressing it on `Property_Age` and extracting the orthogonal residuals — the component of `Property_Age` variation *unexplained* by proximity to tech centers.
- **FWL Validation Regression:** Regressed `Sale_Price` residuals (from the `Distance_to_Tech_Hub` projection) on the partialled `Property_Age` residuals, isolating net effect with all shared covariance stripped.
- **Coefficient Verification:** Confirmed exact numerical equivalence between the FWL-derived coefficient and the multivariate OLS estimate, providing a manual proof of the theorem.

---

## Key Findings

The bivariate model exhibited **severe omitted variable bias (OVB)**: by excluding `Distance_to_Tech_Hub`, the naive regression systematically misattributed inflated market value to `Property_Age`. The coefficient on `Property_Age` was materially distorted — absorbing the premium signal generated by tech hub proximity rather than reflecting the true depreciation effect of structural age.

The multivariate specification corrected this conflation. Critically, the manual FWL procedure — partialling out the shared variance between `Property_Age` and `Distance_to_Tech_Hub` through sequential residual extraction — produced a coefficient **mathematically identical** to the full multivariate OLS estimate. This confirms that OLS, when fully specified, performs implicit orthogonalization across all regressors simultaneously: a geometric decomposition of the covariate space that isolates each variable's net explanatory power.

> In short, what the model "knows" about tech proximity is surgically removed from what it "knows" about property age — and what remains is the true, unconfounded price signal.

---

## Takeaway
The FWL Theorem is not merely a statistical curiosity — it is the formal proof that multivariate OLS achieves *ceteris paribus* through algorithmic dimensionality reduction. Omitting relevant covariates does not neutralize their influence; it launders it into the coefficients of whatever regressors remain.
